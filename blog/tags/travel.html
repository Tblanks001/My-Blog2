I Got Arrested

September 10, 2026 · 4 min read
Travis Blanks
Jack of All Trades

It's been almost a week since I got back from San Antonio for a convention I was selling merch at. During my first attempt of driving there, I got pulled over. The officer initially pulled me over for my window tint being too strong. It escalated into them finding out the car I was driving didn't have registration. I mentioned to them that I was in the process of getting it fixed. The car had to first pass a state inspection before it could be registered. The interaction escalated even more when he saw the I had a salvage title for the vehicle. It turns out I was supposed to have a rebuilt salvage title. Vehicles with salvage titles aren't allowed on the road. The officer told me to step out of the car.

After waiting for about 20 nerve-wracking minutes on the side of the road while the officer was in his vehicle presumably looking up my information, the officer came out of his car and asked me why I have incorrect plates on the car. I confessed to him that it was meant to protect me from being pulled over until I could get the car fixed. The car I bought initially had no plates. It would be foolish to drive around with no plates, so I had to do something until I could get my registration. A prerequisite to getting my registration is passing a state inspection. Because I was reluctant to go to a mechanic and spend hundreds of dollars, I had spent weeks trying to figure out why my car's check engine light won't go away. This whole time I thought I would get away with a warning if I ever got pulled over.

The plan worked for a while. I didn't get pulled over during my trips to New Hampshire and Illinois. That was about 4,000 miles of driving; what's another couple hundred? Thankfully, the officer was lenient on me because I told the truth. He only charged me with driving a vehicle with a salvage title.

The wait in the jail cell was the worst. Because the judge left for the day, I was in there for about 18 hours before I got bail. It's an odd feeling staying in a small, cold room with nothing to do. I spent most of the time just sitting or standing and staring at the brick wall pattern. The graffiti on the wall was also a good source of entertainment. I also got a really good training session in. Here's a bit of what I did:

Single Leg Squat Isometrics (2 sets): Hold a single leg squat (Around parallel depth. If you can't do parallel, go about a quarter of the way down) for 30 seconds. Rest until fully recovered

Single Arm Push-Up Isometrics (2 sets): Hold a single arm push-up (Half-way to the bottom) for 30 seconds. If you can't do one on the ground, place your hand somewhere elevated (and stable so it won't slide away when you put your weight on it) I did this one with my hands on the benches in the cell. Rest until fully recovered.

I've recently changed my training regime to mostly isometrics. Isometrics help improve tendon strength and muscle fiber recruitment. It also helps with bringing blood flow to injured areas. I've been having elbow tendinitis and knee pain. I believe this will help alleviate it and make me stronger. I like trying out new techniques to not only increase my speed, but to also prevent injuries.

It really sucks, but I lost my Hyundai Azera. When I went to the towing company that took my car after I got arrested, they said it would cost $560 to get it out. I decided it wasn't worth that much. I've learned my lesson after trying to revive my Toyota Camry. I will no longer hold onto an old vehicle that isn't worth my time and money.

Luckily, my step mom said I could keep her old car. Although I missed a day, it was still the best I've sold at an anime convention. The new car I'm driving is smaller than the last 2 cars I had, but I believe I can get it travel-ready after a few experiments. I'll show it off once I've gotten it ready.

Handwriting Blogs

August 25, 2026 · 3 min read
Travis Blanks
Jack of All Trades

I'm at a library in Missouri writing this. I'm going to start writing these on paper first before I type it out. I was listening to a podcast yesterday that said physically writing things down is more engaging and creative for your brain. They said it had something to do with your hand muscles being activated, which creates a more hand-on experience (No pun intended). I'm not sure why, but it feels like my hand-writing has improved. It's strange because I haven't written this much since college.

I'm on my way home from an anime convention called Anime Magic in Rosemont, Illinois. I was selling Rectangle Corner controllers there. Out of the three anime conventions I've sold merch at, this was the best one by far. Not only was the foot traffic greater, the attraction to my booth was higher. I took my co-owner's advice and tried playing the game that I have at the booth for people to test out the controllers. He thought it would attract more people to the booth. Since my back was turned away from the passer-bys, I'm not sure how effective that was. I was told by another vendor that this convention had 8-10 thousand attendees, so it may have been a combination of the size and my new strategy. They actually let me drive my car into the convention center to set up and break down.

This is the second convention I've been to sine leaving Texas for my first one two weeks ago. I'm really lucky I got to do this. I spent hours working on the used car I recently bought to get it ready for this trip. After all that work, I still had to work on it a bit more while on the road. Turns out I didn't fully close a valve cover gasket. Halfway through the trip, I noticed oil was spilling in large amounts. I was able to alleviate it a bit, but there is still white smoke and the smell of burning engine oil when I check under the hood.

Fixing cars isn't for the weak of heart. It feels like another problem arises right after I fix an issue. I cried for the first time in years when I thought my car was fixed, only to find out my power steering hose was broken and pouring fluid everywhere. This happened on my first attempt to leave for my trip. I cut it close, but I'm glad I was able to do this. I learned a lot about my car, as well as how far I can push myself to figure out solutions in dire situations.
